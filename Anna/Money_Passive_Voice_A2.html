💡 borrow ≠ lend · spend ≠ waste — watch these pairs!
🔑 Meanings & the tricky pairs
  • borrow = take & give back · lend = give for a while. “I borrow from you / you lend to me.”
  • spend = use money (neutral) · waste = use money badly. earn = get by working · save = keep for later.
  • cash = coins & notes · credit card = pay now, pay back later · cost = the price · pay for + thing (pay for the coffee).
  • Drill idea: say a situation, student says the verb — “You get £2,000 a month…” (earn), “You buy things you don’t need…” (waste), “You keep £50 in the bank…” (save).

Presentation · Grammar

The Passive Voice

Discover + rule 15 min

Look at these pairs of sentences. The meaning is the same — but the focus is different. What changes in the second sentence?

Active — focus on the peoplePeople use credit cards everywhere.
➜
Passive — focus on the cardsCredit cards are used everywhere.
ActiveChina invented paper money.
➜
PassivePaper money was invented in China.
💡
In the passive, the important thing (cards, paper money) comes first. We don’t always say who does the action. The verb becomes two parts: a form of be + the past participle (V3).

❓ When do we use the passive?

We use the passive when the action is more important than who does it, or when we don’t know / don’t need to say who does it.

🤷 We don’t know: My wallet was stolen. 🙄 It’s obvious: The thief was arrested. 📦 Focus on the thing: Coins are made of metal.

🔧 How do we make it?  be + V3

subject  +  be  +  past participle (V3)

Present Simple Passive — am / is / are + V3

Coins are made of metal.
Cash is used less nowadays.

Past Simple Passive — was / were + V3

The first coins were made in Lydia.
My bag was stolen yesterday.

SubjectPresent (be)Past (be)
Iamwas
he / she / it · money, cash (singular)iswas
you / we / they · coins, cards (plural)arewere
🧠
The verb never changes for the subject. Only be changes (is/are/was/were). The main verb always stays as the V3: used, made, paid, invented…

📖 The past participle (V3)

Regular verbs: verb + -ed → used, invented, printed, saved, opened.

Common irregular verbs (money topic) — you must learn these:

make → made
buy → bought
sell → sold
pay → paid
spend → spent
lend → lent
steal → stolen
give → given
take → taken
find → found
build → built
keep → kept

🔁 Active ➜ Passive in 3 steps

Example: “The shop accepts cards.”

Move the object to the front → it becomes the new subject.  Cards…
Add the correct form of be (same tense as the original).  Cards are…
Change the main verb to the V3.  Cards are accepted.
➕
Do you want to say who does the action? Add by + person: Taxes are collected by the government. Use “by …” only when it’s important — often we leave it out.

🕰️ Useful time words

Usually with the PAST passive

centuries ago in the 7th century during the war until 1990

Usually with the PRESENT passive

nowadays these days each month for many years
🎯 Teaching notes & anticipated problems
  • Elicit, don’t tell. Use the discovery box first — ask “What’s different?” before revealing the pattern. Concept-check with timelines (present vs past).
  • CCQs: “Coins are made of metal — do we know WHO makes them? Is it important?” (No / No → that’s why passive.) “Was invented — present or past?” (Past.)
  • Common errors to pre-empt: ① dropping be (*“Cards used everywhere”); ② wrong participle (*“is grow / are use”); ③ subject–be agreement (*“coins is made”); ④ borrow/lend mix-up.
  • Pronunciation: the weak forms — are made /ə(r) ˈmeɪd/, is used /ɪz ˈjuːzd/. Drill the two-part verb as one chunk.

The Money Time Machine

Adventure 15 min

A journey through the whole history of money — from ancient trades to today’s digital payments. Complete a passive sentence at each stop to power the machine and move forward in time. ⭐ Collect all 8 stops!

🎯 Freer production — how to run it
  • Setup: hand the screen to the student. They tap the glowing stop, read the fact aloud, then choose be + V3. Each correct answer opens a 🗣️ speaking prompt (visible in Teacher view) — use it before moving on.
  • Push output: at each era ask “Say it in a full sentence”, then a follow-up (“Why?”, “And in your country?”). Aim for 1–2 minutes of talk per stop.
  • Final challenge (after the trophy): the student re-tells the history of money using the passive — a 5–6 sentence mini-monologue. Great for delayed error correction.
  • If time is short: do stops 1–4 in the lesson, set 5–8 + the monologue as speaking homework.
