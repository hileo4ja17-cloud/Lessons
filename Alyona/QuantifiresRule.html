Much = uncountable. Many = countable plural. A lot of = both.

Much is common in questions and negatives: Do you have much time? Many goes with things we can count: many lessons. A lot of is natural in positive sentences.
much watermany booksa lot of moneya lot of friends

some quantity

Some and any can work with both countable plural and uncountable nouns.

Some is usual in positive sentences and polite requests. Any is usual in questions and negatives.

A little = uncountable. A few = countable plural.

The article a makes the meaning positive: not much, but enough.
a little timea little cheesea few applesa few questions
little / few
small and not enough

Little = uncountable. Few = countable plural.

Without a, the meaning is more negative: not enough.
little moneylittle waterfew friendsfew ideas
a / an / none
one or zero

A / an = one singular countable thing. None = zero.

A before a consonant sound, an before a vowel sound. None means “not one / not any”.
